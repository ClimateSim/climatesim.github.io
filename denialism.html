CLIMATE DENIALISM & SILENCE

Why the defining existential threat of our era rarely leads the news—and why the underlying physics leaves no room for delay.

📰 Crowded Headlines, Missing Emergency

Every day, urgent crises compete for our attention in the headlines: the war in Ukraine, escalating conflict involving Iran, potential existential risks from artificial intelligence alongside the local environmental strain of the data centres being built to power it, fears of economic instability or an AI-bubble collapse, and the relentless cost-of-living squeeze—including the volatile price of fossil fuels themselves.

What rarely makes the front page, however, is the overarching existential threat of human-induced climate change. And when it does break through, media coverage is routinely diluted by familiar talking points—commentators insisting "it's too expensive" to act, or dismissing national responsibility with "we're only 1% of global emissions."

"It's Too Expensive"

Delaying decarbonisation does not save money—it locks households into volatile imported fossil fuel prices while multiplying the far steeper economic cost of extreme weather, flood damage, crop failures, and uninsurable infrastructure.

"We're Only 1%"

No single country can solve a global atmospheric commons alone—yet rich nations built the cumulative industrial carbon debt, outsource heavy manufacturing emissions overseas, and set the technological and diplomatic pace for everyone else.

A Narrowing Window: Credible climate scientists are now warning that humanity has only a few years left to bend the emissions curve downward—to start reducing, rather than still increasing, global greenhouse gas emissions.

🔬 The Science Is Beyond Dispute: Nearly 60 Years of Verified Maths

The physical science is settled and extensively referenced across this site. One of the most striking demonstrations inside the ClimateSim 1D Warming Simulator is that it computes global average surface temperatures directly from measured atmospheric CO₂ concentrations beginning in 1958 (the Mauna Loa Keeling Curve).

Nearly Six Decades of Empirical Agreement: For nearly sixty years, the radiative transfer maths of IPCC AR6 reproduces observed global warming with remarkable accuracy. When a physical equation matches six decades of real-world measurements, it is very difficult to argue that the same physics will suddenly lose accuracy in the future.

⚠️ Dire Projections: Why Even Immediate Net Zero Isn't Instant Safety

The simulator's projections are sobering—both on our current emissions trajectory and even under an idealised Net Zero pathway. Stopping net emissions does not immediately stop warming; because of ocean thermal inertia and committed radiative imbalance, temperature rise slows rather than halting overnight.

Crossing Three Tipping Points Within 50 Years: Even if the world achieved immediate Net Zero from 2026—a scenario that is practically impossible, or at the very least extraordinarily unlikely—the simulator estimates that global warming would still carry the Earth system past the first three climate tipping points less than 50 years from now.

Every fraction of a degree—and every year of delay—determines how many irreversible tipping cascades are triggered and how violently the climate shifts for future generations.

🌀 The Polycrisis & Whose Interests Denial Serves

So the news cycle continually crowds out the existential threat of climate change. It probably will not be a single Hollywood-style catastrophe that begins causing severe loss of human life and biodiversity, but an escalating, compounding volume of simultaneous shocks—crop failures, lethal heatwaves, flash floods, grid strain, insurance collapse, and resource conflict—a polycrisis.

Yet despite the settled science—and the fact that physical models are accurately reproducing real-world warming—in nearly every online thread and too many broadcast debates, the same climate denial and delay tropes keep resurfacing:

"It's Happened Before, Nothing We Can Do"

Natural ice-age cycles unfolded over ~10,000 years; modern industrial warming is occurring ~30× faster. See the 1-Million-Year Comparison.

"No Point Until China / US / India Act"

A classic prisoner's-dilemma excuse for inaction, ignoring cumulative historical emissions, offshored manufacturing, and the fact that clean energy deployment lowers domestic bills regardless.

"The Science Is Uncertain"

It is not. Radiative transfer physics has been verified by laboratory spectroscopy since Tyndall (1859) and matched against 60+ years of Mauna Loa CO₂ and satellite OLR measurements.

Follow the Incentive: Why does this propaganda keep resurfacing? In whose interest is it to keep pumping CO₂ into the atmosphere—driving temperature change over a single century at a pace unseen in Earth's recent geological history, and threatening livelihoods and eventually lives in the millions or potentially billions? The answer lies wherever state budgets, oligarchic power, or corporate windfalls depend on fossil fuel extraction.

🛢️ Russia: Petro-State Power, Gazprom & Geopolitical Distraction

In the early 1990s, the Soviet Union collapsed. Weak political leadership oversaw chaotic privatisation that transferred vast former state industries into private hands. When Boris Yeltsin—the heavy-drinking post-Soviet president—stepped down at the end of 1999, the resulting power vacuum was filled by Vladimir Putin.

Bit by bit, independent democratic checks were dismantled and the post-Soviet oligarchs were brought under Kremlin control; rivals and critics who opposed the system were sidelined, exiled, or eliminated. Crucially, a massive share of Russian state revenue became structurally dependent on fossil fuel exports—anchored by state-backed giants such as Gazprom and Rosneft.

Conflict, Oil Prices & Attention: Independent tracking by the Centre for Research on Energy and Clean Air (CREA) shows Russia's fossil fuel export revenues have exceeded €1 trillion since February 2022, with oil and gas taxes historically funding roughly a quarter to a third of the Russian federal budget. While war in Ukraine has fractured European gas markets, geopolitical instability and energy-price spikes repeatedly divert global political bandwidth and media attention away from the accelerating atmospheric crisis—raising a stark question about who benefits when climate urgency is pushed off the agenda.

🇺🇸 America: Fossil Self-Sufficiency, Corporate Windfalls & Smokescreens

Suddenly in 2026, another major geopolitical distraction dominates the headlines. Meanwhile, the United States—already the world's largest producer of crude oil and natural gas—has achieved domestic self-sufficiency in key fossil fuels, while major American energy corporations have reaped tens to hundreds of billions of dollars in windfall profits amid Middle East instability and conflict legacies stretching back to the Iraq war.

Against that backdrop, Trump-era energy policy has prioritised expanding fossil fuel drilling, rolling back vehicle and power-plant emissions rules, and challenging clean-energy incentives—while U.S. federal tax breaks and subsidies continue to channel tens of billions of dollars annually toward oil and gas production.

A Smokescreen in Front of the Emergency: Does an administration backed by fossil-fuel expansion have every political and financial incentive to throw up fresh geopolitical and cultural smokescreens in front of the climate emergency? Congressional subpoena records and peer-reviewed research already document decades of coordinated industry "roadblocking"—using public-relations pledges and manufactured doubt to delay rapid decarbonisation while record extraction continues.
