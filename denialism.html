CLIMATE DENIALISM & SILENCE

Why the defining existential threat of our era rarely leads the news—and why the underlying physics leaves no room for delay.

📰 Crowded Headlines, Missing Emergency

Every day, urgent crises compete for our attention in the headlines: the war in Ukraine, escalating conflict involving Iran, potential existential risks from artificial intelligence alongside the local environmental strain of the data centres being built to power it, fears of economic instability or an AI-bubble collapse, and the relentless cost-of-living squeeze—including the volatile price of fossil fuels themselves.

What rarely makes the front page, however, is the overarching existential threat of human-induced climate change. And when it does break through, media coverage is routinely diluted by familiar talking points—commentators insisting "it's too expensive" to act, or dismissing national responsibility with "we're only 1% of global emissions."

"It's Too Expensive"

Delaying decarbonisation does not save money—it locks households into volatile imported fossil fuel prices while multiplying the far steeper economic cost of extreme weather, flood damage, crop failures, and uninsurable infrastructure.

"We're Only 1%"

No single country can solve a global atmospheric commons alone—yet rich nations built the cumulative industrial carbon debt, outsource heavy manufacturing emissions overseas, and set the technological and diplomatic pace for everyone else.

A Narrowing Window: Credible climate scientists are now warning that humanity has only a few years left to bend the emissions curve downward—to start reducing, rather than still increasing, global greenhouse gas emissions.

🔬 The Science Is Beyond Dispute: Nearly 60 Years of Verified Maths

The physical science is settled and extensively referenced across this site. One of the most striking demonstrations inside the ClimateSim 1D Warming Simulator is that it computes global average surface temperatures directly from measured atmospheric CO₂ concentrations beginning in 1958 (the Mauna Loa Keeling Curve).

Nearly Six Decades of Empirical Agreement: For nearly sixty years, the radiative transfer maths of IPCC AR6 reproduces observed global warming with remarkable accuracy. When a physical equation matches six decades of real-world measurements, it is very difficult to argue that the same physics will suddenly lose accuracy in the future.

⚠️ Dire Projections: Why Even Immediate Net Zero Isn't Instant Safety

The simulator's projections are sobering—both on our current emissions trajectory and even under an idealised Net Zero pathway. Stopping net emissions does not immediately stop warming; because of ocean thermal inertia and committed radiative imbalance, temperature rise slows rather than halting overnight.

Crossing Three Tipping Points Within 50 Years: Even if the world achieved immediate Net Zero from 2026—a scenario that is practically impossible, or at the very least extraordinarily unlikely—the simulator estimates that global warming would still carry the Earth system past the first three climate tipping points less than 50 years from now.

Every fraction of a degree—and every year of delay—determines how many irreversible tipping cascades are triggered and how violently the climate shifts for future generations.
